POLICY SUMMARY
==============
Bounded benchmark policy for consumer reporting disputes.

1. Consumer reporting agency reinvestigation trigger. If the completeness or
accuracy of an item in a consumer's file is disputed by the consumer and the
consumer reporting agency receives notice directly or through a reseller, the
agency must conduct a reasonable reinvestigation unless a valid frivolous or
irrelevant termination applies. A valid dispute packet identifies the consumer,
identifies the account or item, identifies the disputed field or information,
and explains the basis for the dispute.

2. Time to complete reinvestigation. The agency must complete the
reinvestigation within 30 calendar days after receipt of the dispute notice.
The period may extend to 45 days if the consumer provides additional relevant
information during the initial 30-day period. The extension does not apply
where the agency determines within the initial period that the item is
inaccurate, incomplete, or cannot be verified.

3. Furnisher notice by the agency. Before the end of the fifth business day
after receiving the consumer's dispute, the agency must notify the furnisher
of the disputed information when the item was provided by a furnisher, and the
notice must include all relevant information received from the consumer. Later
relevant information received during the reinvestigation period must be
promptly provided to the furnisher. Primary source documents submitted by the
consumer, such as bank statements, creditor letters, account statements,
police reports, identity theft affidavits, court orders, or billing
statements, are relevant when they bear on the disputed information.

4. Consideration and reasonableness. The agency must review and consider all
relevant consumer information. A reasonable reinvestigation compares the
consumer submission, furnisher response, agency file, prior dispute history
where relevant, and public-record source where relevant. Automated dispute
coding alone is not sufficient when primary documents or material conflicts
require review.

5. Treatment of inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information. If the
item is inaccurate, incomplete, or cannot be verified, the agency must delete
or modify it as appropriate and record the current status. If no defect is
found after a reasonable reinvestigation, the item may remain verified.

6. Results notice. Within 5 business days after completing the
reinvestigation, the agency must provide written results to the consumer. The
notice must state that the reinvestigation is completed, include a consumer
report based on the revised file, disclose the right to request a description
of the procedure used, disclose furnisher contact information if reasonably
available, disclose the right to add a consumer statement, and disclose the
right to request notification of deletion or dispute notation.

7. Reinsertion. Deleted information may not be reinserted unless the furnisher
certifies that it is complete and accurate. If reinserted, the agency must
notify the consumer in writing within 5 business days, identify the furnisher,
provide the consumer's right to add a statement, and maintain reasonable
procedures designed to prevent improper reappearance.

8. Frivolous or irrelevant disputes. The agency may terminate a
reinvestigation if it reasonably determines that the dispute is frivolous or
irrelevant, including because the consumer failed to provide sufficient
information to investigate. The agency must notify the consumer within 5
business days after that determination, provide the reasons, and identify any
information required to investigate.

9. Reseller disputes. A reseller receiving a dispute must determine within 5
business days whether the item is incomplete or inaccurate because of a
reseller act or omission. If so, the reseller must correct or delete within 20
days. If not, the reseller must convey the dispute and all relevant consumer
information to the consumer reporting agency that supplied the information and
reconvey the agency's completion notice to the consumer.

10. Furnisher duties after agency notice. A furnisher receiving agency notice
of a dispute must investigate the disputed information, review all relevant
information provided by the agency, report results to the agency, report
incomplete or inaccurate findings to other nationwide consumer reporting
agencies to which it furnished the information, and modify, delete, or
permanently block information that is inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable.
The furnisher must complete those actions before the agency's applicable
reinvestigation deadline.

11. Furnisher direct disputes. A furnisher receiving a qualifying direct
dispute must investigate when the notice is sent to a proper address, identifies
the account or relationship, identifies the specific disputed information,
explains the basis for the dispute, includes reasonably required supporting
documentation, and concerns covered account or relationship information. The
furnisher need not investigate direct disputes outside the regulatory scope,
including disputes solely about public records, fraud alerts, active duty
alerts, information from another furnisher, or disputes reasonably believed to
be submitted by a credit repair organization. A furnisher that invokes a
frivolous or irrelevant direct-dispute determination must notify the consumer
within 5 business days, state reasons, and identify information needed to
investigate.

12. Consumer statement and subsequent reporting. If the reinvestigation does
not resolve the dispute and the consumer files a brief statement, subsequent
reports containing the disputed information must clearly note that it is
disputed and include the statement or an accurate codification or summary,
unless there are reasonable grounds to believe the statement is frivolous or
irrelevant.

13. Human-review routing. Human review is required when material evidence
conflicts remain unresolved, identity theft or mixed-file evidence is
plausible but incomplete, a court order or legal-liability issue changes the
ordinary fact-verification path, a veteran medical debt claim may require the
special deletion process, primary source documents are missing from the record,
material dates conflict, or the institution's manual review policy is
triggered.
